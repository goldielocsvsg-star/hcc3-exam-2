Sickle Cell Disease, Hemophilia, and Clotting

About 20 questions. This module covers assessment findings, complications, priority interventions, health promotion, medication teaching, lab interpretation, bleeding precautions, and blood product administration for the genetics and clotting content assigned to Exam 2.

THE ONE-LINE VERSION
Sickle cell — autosomal recessive hemoglobin disorder that threatens oxygenation and perfusion. Think oxygenation, hydration, pain.
Hemophilia — usually X-linked recessive clotting factor deficiency that threatens hemostasis. Think bleeding recognition, factor replacement, injury prevention.

SICKLE CELL DISEASE

Genetic Transmission

Autosomal recessive inheritance drives every probability question on this topic.

  • Sickle cell disease is inherited in an autosomal recessive pattern. A person with disease inherits a pathogenic hemoglobin variant from each parent.
  • When both parents have sickle cell trait, every pregnancy carries a 25% chance of disease, a 50% chance of trait, and a 25% chance of neither.
  • These probabilities reset with every pregnancy. A previous child's outcome does not change the next child's risk.
Exam trap: Sickle cell trait is carrier status, not sickle cell disease. Do not label the 50% carrier outcome as "affected with disease."
PUNNETT SQUARE, IN WORDS
25% — Disease (two pathogenic alleles)
50% — Trait / carrier (one pathogenic allele)
25% — Neither disease nor trait

SICKLE CELL DISEASE

Priority Nursing Interventions

The same seven-step framework applies across almost every SCD scenario question.

  1. Assess airway, breathing, oxygen saturation, circulation, temperature, pain, neurologic status, and the patient's usual crisis pattern.
  2. Support oxygenation. Apply O2 when hypoxemia or significant respiratory compromise is present and reassess response. New chest symptoms need urgent escalation.
  3. Restore and maintain hydration with oral or IV fluids as ordered. Monitor I&O, daily weight when indicated, electrolytes, renal function, lung sounds, and fluid overload.
  4. Treat pain promptly. Use prescribed multimodal therapy, reassess pain and sedation, avoid stereotyping or undertreating the patient.
  5. Promote perfusion: rest, warmth, avoid constrictive clothing, keep extremities extended, avoid pressure such as a cuff on a painful limb.
  6. Evaluate fever urgently for infection or sepsis. Obtain ordered CBC, cultures, chest imaging; give antibiotics promptly as prescribed.
  7. Anticipate blood transfusion for selected severe anemia, stroke, acute chest syndrome, or other serious complications as ordered.
Fluids & electrolytes goal: Adequate hydration without overload. Watch urine output, mucous membranes, weight, sodium/potassium, renal function, and respiratory status.
SICKLE CELL DISEASE · MEDICATION

Hydroxyurea Teaching

One of the highest-yield medication-teaching topics in this module.

Teaching pointWhat the patient should understand
PurposeIncreases fetal hemoglobin, decreases sickling and inflammation, reduces pain crises and acute chest syndrome; may reduce transfusions and hospitalizations.
AdministrationTake the prescribed oral dose every day. It does not work well when taken inconsistently.
MonitoringFrequent CBC and dose adjustment, especially early in therapy.
Major adverse effectsMay suppress bone marrow — low WBCs, platelets, or worsening anemia.
Pregnancy safetyTeratogenic. Follow prescriber's contraception/pregnancy prevention instructions; contact provider before pregnancy.
When to reportFever, infection signs, unusual bleeding/bruising, severe fatigue, or other cytopenia symptoms.
SICKLE CELL DISEASE · MEDICATION

Pain Medication Focus

Opioid safety monitoring plus one specific drug to avoid.

Morphine sulfate

Prescribed for severe vaso-occlusive pain. Assess pain before and after dosing; monitor respiratory rate, oxygen saturation, sedation, blood pressure, bowel function, and fall risk.

Naloxone

Should be available per policy for opioid-induced respiratory depression. Do not wait for severe respiratory compromise to escalate concerns.

Avoid meperidine (Demerol) when alternatives are available — normeperidine accumulation can provoke seizures.
HEMOPHILIA & CLOTTING

Core Disorders & Inheritance

Three disorders, one inheritance pattern to master.

DisorderDefectHigh-yield clue
Hemophilia AFactor VIII deficiencyClassic hemophilia; usually X-linked recessive; treat with factor VIII replacement.
Hemophilia BFactor IX deficiency"Christmas disease"; usually X-linked recessive; treat with factor IX replacement.
Von Willebrand diseaseDeficient/dysfunctional vWF; factor VIII may be reducedMucosal bleeding and impaired platelet adhesion; inheritance depends on subtype.
X-LINKED RECESSIVE PATTERN
More males are affected.
There is no father-to-son transmission (fathers pass a Y to sons).
An affected father passes his altered X to all daughters (who become carriers).
A carrier mother has a 50% chance of passing the altered X in each pregnancy.
HEMOPHILIA & CLOTTING

Signs & Symptoms of Bleeding

Hemarthrosis and intracranial bleeding are the two findings tested most often.

  • Easy bruising, petechiae, purpura, prolonged bleeding after cuts, venipuncture, dental work, surgery, or trauma.
  • Epistaxis, gum bleeding, heavy menstrual bleeding, blood in urine or stool, hematemesis, or melena.

Hemarthrosis

Bleeding into a joint causing pain, warmth, swelling, stiffness, decreased range of motion, and a flexed position. Repeated episodes can permanently damage the joint.

Muscle bleeding

Swelling, pain, firmness, decreased motion, and neurovascular compromise.

Intracranial bleeding warning signs: severe headache, vomiting, confusion, seizure, unequal pupils, weakness, or loss of consciousness. Treat head injury as an emergency even without external bleeding.

HEMOPHILIA & CLOTTING

Nursing Care for Active Bleeding

Do not let diagnostics delay factor replacement in a suspected serious bleed.

  1. Assess airway, breathing, circulation, hemodynamic stability, location/amount of bleeding, pain, and neurovascular status. Look for concealed internal bleeding.
  2. Notify the provider or activate emergency response for major bleeding, head or abdominal trauma, airway bleeding, shock, neurologic changes, or uncontrolled hemorrhage.
  3. Administer the prescribed clotting factor promptly. Do not delay factor replacement while waiting on all diagnostics when a serious bleed is suspected and emergency orders are active.
  4. For joint or muscle bleeding: stop activity, rest, ice wrapped in cloth, gentle compression if prescribed, elevation. Do not massage the area.
  5. After venipuncture, apply firm direct pressure longer than usual and reassess for bleeding or hematoma.
  6. Monitor vital signs, pain, mental status, distal circulation, Hgb/Hct, urine output, and response to factor or blood products.
HEMOPHILIA & CLOTTING

Recombinant Factor VIII

Targeted replacement — and it only covers one of the two classic hemophilias.

  • Replaces missing factor VIII in hemophilia A. It does not treat hemophilia B, which requires factor IX replacement.
  • Verify patient, product, prescribed dose, route, venous access, and indication. Administer per product and facility guidance.
  • Monitor whether bleeding, pain, swelling, and range of motion improve. Watch for hypersensitivity and the development of factor inhibitors when therapy becomes less effective.
HEMOPHILIA & CLOTTING

Bleeding Precautions & Education

Everyday injury prevention rules that show up as "which statement indicates understanding" questions.

  • Avoid aspirin and other platelet-impairing medications unless specifically prescribed. Use acetaminophen when appropriate and ordered.
  • Avoid IM injections, rectal temperatures, unnecessary invasive procedures, and contact sports or high-trauma activities.
  • Use a soft toothbrush and consistent preventive dental care. Apply prolonged pressure after needle sticks.
  • Wear medical identification and keep the home factor plan, supplies, emergency contacts, and treatment center information accessible.
  • Seek urgent care after head, neck, abdominal, or major trauma; uncontrolled bleeding; neurologic changes; breathing difficulty; or severe joint swelling and pain.
  • Use genetic counseling before pregnancy or when discussing reproductive risk — accurate, non-directive, confidential, patient-centered.

Excessive Clotting vs. Excessive Bleeding

Risk factors raise suspicion — assessment findings and diagnostics confirm the problem.

FeatureExcessive clottingExcessive bleeding
Common predispositionsPrior VTE, immobility, surgery/trauma, malignancy, pregnancy/estrogen therapy, obesity, smoking, dehydration, central venous catheter, older age, inherited thrombophilia (e.g., factor V Leiden)Hemophilia or von Willebrand disease, thrombocytopenia, liver disease, vitamin K deficiency, anticoagulants/antiplatelets, bone marrow suppression, major trauma, surgery, DIC
Typical findingsUnilateral leg swelling, warmth, redness, tenderness; sudden dyspnea/chest pain; tachycardia; hypoxemia; neuro deficit if arterial flow is blockedBruising, petechiae, mucosal bleeding, prolonged oozing, hemarthrosis, hematuria, GI bleeding, hypotension, tachycardia, pallor, falling hemoglobin
Immediate concernEmbolization or loss of blood flow to lung, brain, heart, or limbHemorrhagic shock or concealed bleeding into brain, airway, chest, abdomen, joint, or muscle
Nursing responseAssess ABCs, oxygenation, perfusion; do not massage a suspected DVT; notify provider; anticipate imaging and anticoagulation as orderedApply direct pressure when appropriate, avoid further trauma, notify provider, obtain ordered labs, give factor/blood products, monitor hemodynamics
Do not confuse risk with diagnosis: risk factors increase suspicion, but assessment findings and diagnostic studies determine the problem. In a bleeding patient, stabilize first and investigate the cause concurrently.
BIG PICTURE

Blood Product Administration

Safety checklist plus a quick reference for which product does what.

  1. Verify the order, indication, consent, patient identity, blood component, compatibility, expiration, and required baseline assessment per policy.
  2. Use approved blood tubing and normal saline. Follow facility policy for infusion pumps, rates, independent verification, and maximum hang time.
  3. Obtain baseline vital signs, remain with the patient during the initial high-risk period, and reassess vitals at required intervals.
  4. Watch for fever, chills, flushing, rash, itching, dyspnea, wheezing, chest/back pain, anxiety, nausea, hypotension, tachycardia, or dark urine.
  5. If a transfusion reaction is suspected: stop the blood immediately, keep the vein open with new tubing and normal saline, assess ABCs and vitals, notify provider and blood bank, follow policy for specimens/documentation. Do not restart the unit.
ProductPrimary use in this module
Packed RBCsIncrease oxygen-carrying capacity after significant blood loss or severe anemia. Do not replace the missing clotting factor.
PlateletsTreat thrombocytopenia or platelet dysfunction. Not routinely indicated for uncomplicated factor VIII or IX deficiency.
Fresh frozen plasmaProvides multiple clotting factors when broad replacement is needed and a specific concentrate is unavailable or not indicated.
CryoprecipitateProvides fibrinogen, factor VIII, factor XIII, and von Willebrand factor; used mainly for low fibrinogen per orders/policy.
Recombinant factor concentratePreferred targeted replacement for the specific deficiency, e.g., recombinant factor VIII for hemophilia A.
BIG PICTURE

Genetic Counseling Essentials

The "non-directive" theme applies across every genetics topic on this exam.

  • Collect a three-generation family history when possible: disease, carrier status, early deaths, bleeding history, relevant ethnicity/ancestry, and consanguinity.
  • Explain inheritance probabilities clearly. Use "with each pregnancy" — risk resets every time.
  • Remain non-directive. Provide education, testing information, referrals, counseling, and support without telling the patient what reproductive decision to make.
  • Protect privacy and confidentiality, assess coping and psychological safety, and confirm understanding using teach-back.
